1. What This App Is

The Paid Marketing Performance Dashboard is an operational decision tool for campaign managers and general managers. It answers a single question every week: Are we spending the right amount on each brand to hit our revenue and efficiency targets?

Weekly Monitoring

Track revenue, spend, and MTS (Marketing-to-Sales ratio) at the vertical, brand, and campaign level. Compare week-over-week, month-over-month, or year-over-year.

Budget Accountability

Compare actual performance against monthly budgets. Identify brands that are overspending, underspending, or pacing off-target.

Campaign Visibility

Drill from vertical-level totals down to individual campaigns. See exactly where spend is going and which campaigns are driving results.

This is not a financial accounting system. Revenue figures come from uploaded CSV data and ad-platform reports. They are best used for week-to-week directional decisions, not for invoice reconciliation or financial close.

Net Revenue vs New Revenue

Net Revenue Revenue after cancellations, returns, and chargebacks. This is the backend source of truth. Use it as the default for performance reviews.
New Revenue Revenue before cancellations. Useful for marketing pacing because it reflects demand generated this period, before downstream fallout. Toggle between the two using the Revenue selector on the Overview page.

2. Core Concepts

Vertical A top-level business segment (e.g., "Home & Garden"). Verticals group brands for budgeting and reporting.
Brand A P&L-owning entity within a vertical. Budgets are set per brand. Every campaign belongs to exactly one brand.
Campaign An individual ad-platform campaign (Google Ads, Meta, Amazon, etc.). Campaigns carry daily spend, click, and conversion data.
Ad Group A subdivision within a campaign. When campaign media CSV includes an “Ad group” column, the drill-down shows one row per ad group with its own metrics. Multi-brand campaigns (where different ad groups target different brands) are automatically split so each brand sees only its own ad group data.
MTS Marketing-to-Sales ratio = Spend ÷ Revenue. An MTS of 25% means you spend $0.25 for every $1 of revenue. Lower is more efficient, but too low may mean you are under-investing and leaving revenue on the table.
MTS Budget The target MTS ratio set in the Budgets page. Brands within ±50 basis points of target are "on track." Brands significantly below target are "Overly Efficient" (potential scale opportunity). Brands above target are "Under Efficient" (spending too much for the revenue generated).
Current vs Compare Every view compares two time windows. The Current period is what you selected (e.g., This Week). The Compare period is an equal-length window shifted back by the chosen comparison: WOW (week-over-week), MOM (month-over-month), or YOY (year-over-year, 52-week offset).
Platform Revenue
(below Brand)		 When you drill into a brand and see Source, Campaign Type, or Campaign rows, the Revenue column shows ad-platform conversion value, not backend revenue. This is because backend revenue (from order systems) only exists at the brand level, not per campaign. Platform revenue will generally not sum to the brand-level figure.
{# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #} {# 3. FIRST-TIME SETUP #} {# ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════ #}

3. First-Time Setup

Complete these steps once before relying on weekly reporting. The dashboard cannot produce meaningful results until brands, campaign data, and budgets are in place.
Create Verticals
Go to Verticals and add your business segments.
Create or Upload Brands
Go to Brands. Add brands one by one, or upload a CSV with brand_name, brand_id, and optionally vertical columns. Assign a Brand ID to each brand so campaigns auto-map during data upload.
Upload Site ID Mapping
Go to Site ID Mapping. Upload a CSV mapping backend site IDs (numeric) to verticals. This is required before uploading revenue data.
Upload Campaign Media Data
Go to Campaign Data. Upload CSV exports from Google Ads, Bing Ads, Meta Ads, or Amazon Marketplace. The system auto-creates campaigns it hasn't seen before. Include the “Ad group” column in your export to enable per-ad-group drill-down metrics.
Verify Campaign-to-Brand Assignments
Go to Match Campaigns. Any auto-created campaigns are initially assigned to "Unknown." Reassign them to the correct brand. If your campaigns use the brand_id; naming convention, many will auto-assign.
Enter Annual Budgets
Go to Budgets. Select a vertical and year, then enter the monthly revenue budget, MTS target, and cancellation rate. Brand-level budgets are auto-allocated based on prior-year revenue share, or can be manually overridden.
Sanity-Check the Overview
Go to Overview. Confirm that brands appear with data, charts render, and alert classifications look reasonable. If brands show "Missing Budget" or "Missing Revenue" alerts, revisit the steps above.

4. Weekly Campaign Manager Workflow

Every week (typically Monday or Tuesday after weekend data finalizes), follow this routine:

Step 1: Open the Overview

Navigate to Overview. Set the filters to This Week / WOW and your preferred revenue type (Net is the default). If you manage a specific vertical, select it from the Vertical dropdown.

Step 2: Read the Top Charts

  • Revenue chart: Is the current line above or below the compare line? A widening gap means things are accelerating (good) or deteriorating (bad).
  • Spend chart: Is spend tracking proportionally with revenue? If spend is up but revenue is flat, efficiency is declining.
  • MTS chart: Is the current MTS within the budget band (dashed lines)? If it's above the upper bound, you're overspending relative to revenue. Below the lower bound, you may be under-investing.

Step 3: Review the Brand Performance Table

Scan the table for color-coded alert badges:

Step 4: Drill Down (When Needed)

Click a brand row to expand it and see Source-level, then Campaign Type-level, then Campaign-level breakdowns. At the campaign level, each row represents an individual ad group (when ad-group data was included in the CSV upload). This lets you see exactly which ad group within a shared campaign is driving spend and conversions. If no ad group data was uploaded, the Ad Group column shows “—” and metrics reflect the whole campaign.

Drill down when: a brand's alert status doesn't match your expectation, or you need to find which specific campaigns or ad groups to pause or scale. Don't drill down when: the brand is "Doing Well" and you just need a quick status check.

Step 5: Take Action

The primary action from this dashboard is budget reallocation: shift spend from Under Efficient brands toward Overly Efficient brands. The goal each week is to pace toward your MTS targets and monthly revenue goals. Make the actual budget changes in your ad platforms, then confirm the impact next week.

5. Weekly Optimization & Scoring

The Weekly Optimization page scores each campaign on scalability (0–100) and recommends specific bid adjustments. Only campaigns with an actionable recommendation are shown—campaigns in a “hold” state are excluded from the table.

Scalability Score (0–100)

A weighted composite of three components. The score determines the traffic-light badge color and the direction/magnitude of the recommendation.

70–100 High scalability. The campaign has room to grow. Recommendation: decrease tROAS (bid more aggressively) to capture incremental revenue.
40–69 Moderate. Campaigns in this range only appear if a seasonality adjustment is triggered (see below); otherwise they are excluded as “hold.”
0–39 Low scalability. Efficiency or budget constraints suggest pulling back. Recommendation: increase tROAS (bid less aggressively) to improve efficiency.

Score Components

A — Elasticity Measures how responsively clicks increase when spend increases (log-log regression over the Elasticity Window). A high elasticity means the campaign can absorb more spend productively. Default weight: 40%.
B — Budget Binding Proxy for budget headroom. Compares the campaign's current spend against an implied budget derived from last year's spend share within the vertical. A low utilization rate means room to scale. Default weight: 30%.
C — Efficiency Stability Weekly ROAS and CVR coefficient of variation, shrunk toward the peer group (same Source × Campaign Type within the vertical). Stable efficiency means the campaign is predictable and safe to scale. Default weight: 30%.

Weights and window sizes can be adjusted on the Scoring Configuration page.

Recommendations

Type When It Fires What It Means
Decrease tROAS by X% Score ≥ 70 Lower the target ROAS to bid more aggressively. The campaign has high elasticity and/or budget headroom. Magnitude is capped at ±15% per week.
Increase tROAS by X% Score < 40 Raise the target ROAS to pull back spend. The campaign is constrained by budget, volatile efficiency, or low elasticity. Magnitude is capped at ±15% per week.
Seasonality Adjustment +X% Score ≥ 60 and vertical revenue pacing < 90% of goal and MTS pacing ≤ 110% of goal A temporary bid boost to help the vertical catch up on revenue pacing. Use when mid-month revenue is behind but efficiency is still acceptable. Remove after the pacing gap closes.

Reason Badges

Each campaign row shows color-coded reason badges that explain why the recommendation was made:

High Elasticity Component A ≥ 0.7 — clicks scale well with additional spend.
Low Elasticity Component A ≤ 0.3 — diminishing returns on incremental spend.
Budget Headroom Component B ≥ 0.7 — campaign is well below its implied budget.
Budget Constrained Component B ≤ 0.3 — campaign is at or over its implied budget.
Stable Efficiency Component C ≥ 0.7 — ROAS and CVR are consistent week-to-week.
Volatile Efficiency Component C ≤ 0.3 — high week-to-week variance in efficiency.
Scale Opportunity Score ≥ 70 — tROAS decrease recommended.
Efficiency Concern Score < 40 — tROAS increase recommended.
Rev Pacing Behind Seasonality adjustment triggered due to vertical revenue pacing behind goal.

MTS Column

The MTS column shows each campaign's Marketing-to-Sales ratio (Spend ÷ Platform Conversion Value) for the selected analysis period. This uses the same period filter applied to the rest of the table. Since this is campaign-level data, the denominator is platform-reported conversion value, not backend revenue (which only exists at the brand level).

Revenue at Risk

A Revenue at Risk badge appears on a brand group header when the brand's projected monthly revenue (based on MTD actuals) falls short of its budget goal. A vertical-level alert banner appears at the top of the page when the entire vertical is projected to miss its revenue goal while also exceeding its MTS target.

6. Weekly Report

The Weekly Report page generates a structured performance summary for each vertical on a weekly basis. It combines auto-filled metrics with manager-written commentary into a single document suitable for stakeholder review.

Getting Started

  1. Select a Vertical and a Week Ending date (Sunday), then click Load.
  2. The Executive Snapshot and Performance by MFG table populate automatically from your campaign and revenue data.
  3. Fill in the commentary sections below the table.
  4. Click Save Draft to persist your work. You can return and edit at any time.
  5. Click Download Word Doc to export the report as a formatted .docx file.

Executive Snapshot

Five scorecards displayed in a row, following Stephen Few's information dashboard design principles:

Revenue Total net revenue for the week with WoW and YoY change indicators. Green arrow = growth, red arrow = decline.
Spend Total media spend for the week with WoW and YoY directional indicators.
MTS Marketing-to-Sales ratio with a bullet graph comparing actual to goal. The bar color indicates target status: green (±50 bps of goal), yellow (±75 bps), red (beyond ±75 bps).
Top Revenue Driver The brand generating the most revenue this week, with its dollar amount.
Largest YoY Shift The brand with the biggest absolute year-over-year revenue change, with the dollar delta.

Performance by MFG Table

Shows the top 5 brands by revenue plus any brands flagged as Needs Attention or Pacing Risk. Each row includes Rev WoW%, Rev YoY%, Spend YoY%, MTS, alert badges, and a notes field for per-brand commentary.

Commentary Sections

Nine structured sections guide the weekly narrative:

Summary Statement — 3–4 sentence overall performance summary
Major YoY Shifts — Key year-over-year changes with dollar amounts and causes
What’s Working Well — Wins, positive trends, and strong performers
What Needs Attention — Issues, underperformance, and flags
What We’re Doing About It — Actions being taken to address issues
Platform Testing & Experiments — Active tests, early results, and next steps
Channel Mix Observations — Platform-level spend allocation and shifts
Risk & Opportunity Outlook — Forward-looking risks and opportunities (2–4 weeks)
GM Discussion Points — Topics requiring GM review or approval
Each commentary field includes placeholder examples showing the expected format and level of detail. Drafts are saved per vertical per week—you can save, leave, and return to continue editing.

6. Weekly GM / Vertical Owner Walkthrough

This section is for general managers and vertical owners who review performance in weekly meetings. The goal is a 10-minute review that answers four questions in order:

Question 1: Are we up or down at the vertical level?

Where to look: Overview page, Revenue and Spend charts.
What to say: "Revenue is [up/down] [X]% week-over-week. Spend is [up/down] [X]%." If both move in the same direction by similar amounts, efficiency is stable. If revenue is down but spend is up, flag it.

Question 2: Are we within MTS targets?

Where to look: MTS chart. The dashed lines show the budget band (±50 bps).
What to say: "Our blended MTS is [X]% against a [Y]% target. We are [on target / above / below]." Above target means overspending; below means potential room to invest more.

Question 3: Which brands are driving the change?

Where to look: Brand Performance table, sorted by the delta columns.
What to say: "Brand A is up 15% WOW and contributing most of the growth. Brand B is down 20% and dragging the vertical." Focus on the two or three brands with the largest absolute revenue deltas.

Question 4: Which brands need action?

Where to look: Alert badges in the Brand Performance table.
What to say:

  • "Brand C is flagged Needs Attention—we should investigate the revenue drop and decide whether to pause campaigns or adjust bids."
  • "Brand D is Overly Efficient—we should test increasing daily budgets by 10–15% to capture incremental revenue."
  • "Brand E is Under Efficient—we should pull back spend and reallocate to better performers."

Wrap up by confirming who owns each action item and when the changes will be live. Use the PDF export to share the snapshot with stakeholders who weren't in the meeting, or use the Weekly Report to produce a structured Word document with auto-filled metrics and your commentary.

7. Access Control & User Groups

All pages require login. Access is controlled by group membership—each user belongs to one or more groups, and each page has a defined access level per group.

User Groups

Campaign Manager Day-to-day campaign operators. Full access to data upload, optimization, and reporting. Read-only on budgets, verticals, and brands.
General Manager Vertical owners. Full access to budgets, verticals, and brands. Read-only on weekly reports. No access to data upload or scoring.
Admin Full access to all pages including user management. Cannot access Django admin directly.
Reporting Similar to Campaign Manager. Full access to uploads and reporting. Read-only on budgets, verticals, brands, and scoring.
Agency External agency users. Full access to uploads and optimization. Read-only on budgets, revenue data, verticals, brands, and weekly reports. No access to site mapping or scoring.
SuperUser Full access to everything, including Django admin and user management.

Access Levels

Full Access View pages and submit forms (GET + POST). All buttons and forms are visible.
Read Only View pages but cannot submit forms. Save/submit buttons are hidden and POST requests are blocked by the middleware. A “Read-only access” banner appears on applicable pages.
No Access Page returns a 403 error. The nav link is hidden.
Users with multiple group memberships receive the most permissive access level across their groups. For example, a user in both Campaign Manager (read-only on Budgets) and Admin (full on Budgets) gets full access to Budgets.

8. Common Pitfalls & How to Interpret Data

What You See Why It Happens
Platform revenue doesn't match backend revenue At the brand level, revenue comes from your order/revenue system (CSV upload). Below the brand level (Source, Campaign Type, Campaign), revenue is the ad platform's last-click conversion value. These are different data sources with different attribution models. They are not expected to match.
A campaign shows spend but $0 revenue The ad platform did not attribute any conversions to that campaign in the selected period. This is common for upper-funnel campaigns (Display, Video) that assist conversions but don't get last-click credit. It does not mean the campaign is worthless.
"Overly Efficient" feels like a good thing Being well below your MTS target means your cost-per-dollar-of-revenue is low—but it also means you may be under-investing. If a brand can profitably absorb more spend, leaving budget on the table means leaving revenue on the table. The alert is a prompt to test scaling up.
Partial-period numbers look low Presets like "This Week" and "This Month" only include data through yesterday. Early in the week or month, totals will be small. The comparison period is trimmed to the same number of elapsed days (pacing), so deltas remain meaningful.
"Missing Revenue" or "Missing Budget" alerts Revenue data hasn't been uploaded for this brand/period, or budgets haven't been entered. These are data-quality alerts, not performance alerts. Upload the missing data or enter budgets to resolve them.
MTS shows "—" for a brand Revenue is zero for the selected period (no CSV data uploaded, or zero orders). MTS is undefined when revenue is zero—the dashboard shows a dash instead of infinity or zero.
Ad Group column shows "—" in drill-down The CSV used for the upload did not include an “Ad group” column, so metrics are stored at the campaign level. Re-upload your ad platform export with the Ad Group column included (e.g., add “Ad group” as a segment in Google Ads) to see per-ad-group breakdowns.
